## Not found / doubts

1. **ChatGPT's launch day now has a page.** TechCrunch's 30 November 2025 anniversary piece is the source for 2022-11-30. Wikipedia's ChatGPT article says only "November 2022" in its text, so one page carries the day, not two.
2. **Microsoft's 2019 post moved.** The original `blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2019/07/22/...` returns 404 and its archive copy 404s too. The `news.microsoft.com/source/2019/07/22/` copy opened and states "$1 billion". The 2023 extension post still says only "multiyear, multibillion dollar", so no ~$10bn figure is claimed.
3. **The Microsoft board seat of November 2023 is still wrong as written.** The Guardian's 17 and 25 November 2023 pages do not say Microsoft got a non-voting seat. Wikipedia says Microsoft "had also attempted to gain a seat" and describes a "non-voting board observership" that the UK regulator looked at. The row should read "Microsoft sought a board seat; an observer arrangement was reported", or be cut.
4. **Two dates moved a day.** Nvidia's memo is dated 24 November 2025 (Business Insider and Cryptopolitan), not 25. Oracle's downgrade is dated 9 July 2026 (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel), not 16 July, which is the report date. Both original rows should change.
5. **Gemini 4 is two weeks and one month out.** Google's release notes put 4 Argon at 13 October 2026; Wikipedia's Gemini infobox says 30 September 2026. The Carbon and Hydrogen posts are November 2026, so the row marked "2026-10" is wrong by a month. The "November 30, 2026" string on the Carbon page noted in 01's doubts was not resolved.
6. **Oracle's credit numbers are two different events.** 203 basis points on Monday 20 July 2026 (Herald Business, Seoul Economic Daily at 2.03pp) is the 18-year high. Wikipedia's AI bubble article gives a record close of "261 basis points" on 8 October 2026. They should not be merged into one point. Bloomberg's own 20 July story refused fetches; its archive copy returned 403.
7. **Anthropic's Series D has two answers and neither is Anthropic's.** $12.5bn pre-money from the certificate of incorporation (Prime Unicorn Index, 1 February 2024) against $18.4bn post-money in press reports (Tech Funding News, June 2024). Both are recorded; nobody should average them. Anthropic published no release for that round.
8. **Anthropic's 2021 and 2022 valuations are still not primary.** The two Anthropic releases give round sizes ($124m, $580m) and no valuation. The $550m (2021) and $4.1bn (2022) figures come from StockAnalysis, an aggregator.
9. **The Anthropic S-1 itself was never read.** sec.gov refuses the 14 MB prospectus ("Response too large", over the 5 MB fetch limit). Revenue and loss figures used elsewhere in the research still come from Wikipedia's table and TechCrunch, not from the filing. The fee table and the cover pages did open.
10. **OpenAI 2019-2022 valuations are still thin.** 2021 exists only as "about $14 billion after a 2021 tender offer" on a valuation-history site. No 2019, 2020 or 2022 valuation was found on any page opened. The $29bn figure is Wikipedia's, undated on the page except by context.
11. **xAI's $230bn is one newsletter.** x.ai's own Series E release gives $20bn and no valuation. The Wikipedia xAI table (now "SpaceXAI") gives pre- and post-money for 2023-2025 but cites its own references, so it is a secondary source.
12. **Nvidia's losses are measured two ways.** Wikipedia's scandal article says Nvidia lost "US$1.5 trillion in market capitalization within over a month". The Guardian's 12 November page says about $180bn went on the day and the company was valued $4tn before it. Different windows; do not add them.
13. **The two Anthropic share counts do not match and no page explains why.** 825,000,000 shares in the roadshow notice against 948,750,000 in the filing-fee table (including a 123,750,000-share underwriters' option). The extra 123,750,000 does not close the gap, so the difference is probably selling holders, but no opened page says so.
14. **Sequoia's 2023 title could not be confirmed.** The brief calls it "AI's $200B Question"; the page at that URL is titled "Follow the GPUs" and states a "$125B+ hole". The $200bn figure appears on no opened page, so the older title is not asserted here.
