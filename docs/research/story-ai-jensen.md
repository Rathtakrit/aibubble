# Jensen Huang: resignation, investigation and reinstatement, November 2026

Research note, 25 November 2026. One question: do the reports say Nvidia's Jensen Huang was removed or stepped
aside, that an investigation looked at a reported honeypot or spying case and reported affairs, and that he was
later reinstated? Only pages opened in this run are used. Words in quotation marks are the page's words; "..." marks
omitted words, and a quotation inside a quotation is shown in single quotes. Nothing here decides whether a private
allegation is true.

**Verdict**

1. He left: Nvidia's board announced on 12 November 2026 that Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board
   member, effective immediately, and named Colette Kress interim CEO.
2. He returned: the board reinstated him unanimously on 23 November 2026, eleven days later, after saying on
   17 November that it did not intend to reinstate him.
3. The investigation has no published result: no opened page reports a finding or an exoneration, the board says
   its inquiry "will proceed to conclusion", and the spying and further affair claims remain allegations.

**Labels**

- **CONFIRMED**: two or more independent outlets say it, or an Nvidia filing or newsroom page says it.
- **SINGLE**: one outlet only.
- **NOT FOUND**: no opened page says it. The row gives the nearest page and quote.
- **ALLEGATION**: a claim by officials, a politician or unnamed sources, not a finding. It is not repeated here as
  fact.

## What no opened page says

- No page says Huang was fired, suspended or put on leave. Nvidia's release says he resigned, and the board chair
  told investors he "voluntarily stepped down". The BBC's "forced to resign" and the Guardian's "ouster" are press
  descriptions of the same event, not a second official account.
- No page publishes the board committee's findings. The 23 November release says the inquiry continues, and a
  Democratic congressman, Raja Krishnamoorthi, said "neither the government nor Nvidia's own board has completed
  its investigation" (The Verge, 23 November).
- No page shows a court, regulator or the board clearing Huang of the spying allegation. Trump's remark that the
  attorney general considered him "exonerated" is the president's account; the Justice Department gave no
  on-the-record answer to The Verge.

## Where the sources differ

- **Removed or resigned.** Nvidia, The Verge and TechCrunch describe a resignation the board accepted. The BBC
  says "forced to resign"; the Guardian uses "ouster" and "ousted". Only the BBC uses "forced". The formal record
  is a resignation, so a story should not say the board fired him without naming who says so.
- **Two affairs or three.** The BBC (12 November, updated 23 November) says "allegations of two affairs". The
  Guardian (17 November) says Huang admitted one and that "reports uncovered two more alleged affairs with company
  employees". TechCrunch says "at least two". The count is not settled by the opened pages.
- **"Honeypot".** The word comes from Senator Josh Hawley, not from an investigator or a filing. It is his
  characterisation of the case (The Verge, 23 November).
- **The 23 November share move.** The Guardian says 32% and "more than $1tn"; its own timeline says 33%; the BBC
  says 31% and "nearly $1 trillion"; TechCrunch says shares "opened 30% higher" and were "up 27%" at 9:51 a.m. ET.
  All agree on a sharp rise of roughly 30% and about $1tn added. The exact figure depends on the outlet and the
  time of day.
- **The 12 November share move** rests on one page, the Guardian report of that day. It returned HTTP 403 when
  reopened on 25 November, so the quote above comes from the first open in this run and could not be checked a
  second time.
- **The SEC investigation has two reported scopes.** The BBC says Reuters reported a probe into "accounting
  practices", unconfirmed by the BBC. The Guardian says the Wall Street Journal reported a probe into "its business
  in China". Both are reported, not confirmed by the SEC.
- **A detail left out.** The Guardian's 12 November report also said the board appointed Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen &
  Katz. That page could not be reopened, so the exact sentence is not quoted and no row relies on it.
