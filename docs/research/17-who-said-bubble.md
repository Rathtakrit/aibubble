# Who said AI is (or is not) a bubble, 2023 to November 2026

Research date: 15 November 2026. A dated record of statements by named people and institutions about whether AI is
a bubble. Quotes only. No view on who is right, no advice, no prediction.

**How the rows were gathered.** Search was by fetching DuckDuckGo result pages
(`https://html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=...`, and `lite.duckduckgo.com` when that endpoint was rate-limited), then
opening the pages that carry the sentence. A row exists only where an opened page prints the words. About 60 pages
were fetched; roughly 50 returned readable content, and the refusals are listed in the doubts list at the end. Where
the original refused this machine, an archived copy was opened and the row says "archive copy".

**Reading the tables.** "Side" is one of three: *says bubble*, *says not a bubble*, *mixed*. It records what the
speaker said about AI, not what the researcher thinks. A warning that valuations are stretched, without the word
"bubble", is marked *says bubble* and the "where" cell says it is a valuation warning. "Checked" says whether the page
named in the URL was opened directly ("opened") or through `web.archive.org` ("archive copy").

**Plain words, first use.**
- *Bubble*: prices or spending far above what the underlying business earns, on the belief that prices will keep
  rising. A bubble ends in a fall.
- *Valuation*: what a share or a company is judged to be worth, often stated as a multiple of earnings.
- *Price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio*: the share price divided by profit per share for a year. A high number means buyers
  are paying more for each dollar of profit.
- *Capex (capital expenditure)*: money spent on long-lived assets — buildings, chips, servers, network gear.
- *Hyperscaler*: the largest cloud and data-centre operators (here: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle).
- *Depreciation*: the yearly charge that spreads the cost of an asset over the years it is used. A longer assumed
  life means a smaller yearly charge and higher reported profit.
- *Credit spread*: the extra interest a borrower pays over a safe government bond. A narrow spread means lenders see
  little risk.

   ineteconomics.org (403); letsdatascience.com (403); reuters.com, bloomberg.com, wsj.com, ft.com, cnbc.com direct
   (refused; one CNBC page was read as an archive copy).
2. **Jim Chanos.** Search found only titles and summaries ("The AI Bubble Is 'Much Worse' Than Dot-Com", Risk Reversal
   podcast, July 2026) and a newsletter's paraphrases. No opened page printed a verbatim sentence from him that also
   carried a date and a full attribution, so he has no row.
3. **Jeremy Grantham's own words.** Bloomberg's 28 February 2025 podcast item and the advisorperspectives report of it
   were found in search results, but neither page was opened with a verbatim quote. The GMO letter in Table A is by
   Ben Inker, not Grantham; Grantham is named only as GMO's co-founder.
4. **2023 statements.** The brief covers 2023 onwards, but no opened page carried a 2023-dated verbatim bubble quote.
   Gary Marcus's newsletter says he warned about the economics of generative AI and a possible bubble in "a pair of
   articles in August 2023", but those posts were not opened, so there is no 2023 row.
5. **David Solomon's "not smart enough" line** appears in Fortune's own framing; the words in Table A are the two
   sentences Fortune puts inside quotation marks. Fortune credits the original reporting to the Financial Times.
6. **Jamie Dimon.** Fortune's body text was opened and quoted. A widely circulated shorter line ("some of the money
   poured into the industry would 'probably be lost'") was seen only in the BBC's report of a separate October 2025
   interview, not printed on a page opened here in direct form, so it is not used as a row.
7. **Jensen Huang's earnings-call quote** (Nvidia's November 2025 results call) was not captured: the page fetched for
   it timed out. The row uses the CNBC "Squawk Box" interview as reported by Windows Central, published 10 October
   2025.

   missing from Table B.

   peak; Fortune says "about 40%" against 20% a decade earlier; a Seeking Alpha summary seen in search results said
   38.7%. Only the first two were opened and are listed; the 38.7% figure is not used.
10. **BIS.** The December 2025 Quarterly Review page was opened and quoted as a valuation warning, but the word

    because the statement is about stretched valuations — not because the BIS used the word.

    itself was not opened.

    output file was created or changed.
