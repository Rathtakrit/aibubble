### What keeps coming back

- **The circle.** Nvidia invests in OpenAI, OpenAI buys Nvidia systems, Oracle and CoreWeave borrow to build for OpenAI, and Microsoft and SoftBank sit on both sides. Every serious piece from late 2025 on is a version of this diagram (Bloomberg's two graphics, DW, the trade press).
- **Depreciation and the life of a chip.** Burry's claim — that spreading a GPU's cost over five or six years flatters profits — became the standard bear argument because it is checkable in filings, and Nvidia felt it had to answer in writing (2025-11-25).
- **Capacity announcements as news.** $500bn Stargate (2025-01-21), $300bn Oracle contract (2025-09-10), $100bn Nvidia letter of intent (2025-09-22), $250bn Azure commitment (2025-10-28). The numbers are commitments and letters of intent, not money spent, and most coverage does not say so in the headline.
- **The gap between revenue and commitment.** OpenAI's annualised revenue (~$50bn, 2026-10-08) against hundreds of billions of purchase commitments is the single most repeated comparison of 2026.
- **Credit, not equities, as the warning light.** From mid-2026 the story moves to Oracle's credit default swaps, junk-rating risk and AI-related bond issuance.
- **The political turn.** Data-centre moratoriums on state ballots, electricity prices, and the 2026 midterms as the trigger for the November sell-off. This is new in 2026 and almost absent from the 2023-2025 bubble literature.
- **"It is not a bubble, it is a build-out."** The other side is consistent too: CNN's charts, Nvidia's memo, UBS's "market indigestion" line, and the argument that buildouts always look circular while they are being financed.

### What the existing visual pieces do well

- Bloomberg's graphics put *arrows* between named companies with dollar figures on them; that is exactly the grammar this site needs.
- CNN's four-chart piece and Statista's capex chart do the "one number, one chart, one decade" job and are readable at phone width.
- The IMF/BIS reports supply the macro frame — AI as a share of GDP growth — which no company graphic can show.

### What none of them shows

- **Time.** Almost all of these are snapshots of the current deal web. None runs a day-by-day slider from 2017, so none shows the *sequence*: paper, product, capex, debt, downgrade.
- **Both lanes at once.** The science pieces (model launches) and the money pieces (deals) are published by different desks and never share an axis, so the reader cannot see that Gemini 3 (2025-11-18) and the credit warnings (2026) sit two quarters apart.
- **The cancelled and delayed things.** Oracle's force majeure on the Jupiter campus, Microsoft trimming its 2026 capex on slipping third-party capacity, and the 2026 IPO wobble are reported once and then dropped from the graphics.
- **Who is exposed.** The "$3.2tn wiped" number is not broken down by who owns it (pension funds, retail index funds, private credit). Only the Guardian's explainer even raises it.
- **Sourcing per number.** The commitment figures ($500bn, $300bn, $100bn) are repeated everywhere without a link to the filing or release that created them; this site's rule is that each value carries its source.
