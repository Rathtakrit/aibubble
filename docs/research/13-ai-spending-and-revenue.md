# AI spending and AI revenue, 2022 to November 2026

Research date: 15 November 2026. Facts only: money going into AI and money coming out. No view on whether the
spending pays off.

**How the numbers were gathered.** Search was by fetching DuckDuckGo result pages, then opening the pages that
reported the figure. `html.duckduckgo.com` answered with a bot challenge on this machine, so the same engine's
`lite.duckduckgo.com` endpoint was used. Company figures come from the companies' own earnings releases (SEC Form 8-K
exhibit 99.1), their investor pages, their SEC filings and the SEC's XBRL company data. Where a page refused to open,
the refusal is listed in the doubts list at the end.

**Plain words, first use.**
- *Capital expenditure (capex)*: money a company spends on long-lived assets — buildings, servers, chips, network
  gear. It is not an expense in the year it is spent; it is spread over the years the asset is used.
- *Finance lease*: renting an asset in a way that is counted like buying it. Accounting rules put the whole lease on
  the books at the start, so some companies report capex "including finance leases", which is bigger than the cash
  they actually paid.
- *Remaining performance obligation (RPO)*: signed contracts not yet counted as revenue. It is a backlog number.
- *Annualised revenue run rate*: one month's or one quarter's revenue multiplied up to a year. It is not revenue
  booked over a year, and companies calculate it differently from each other.

Every figure is printed on the page named in its row, or is the sum of parts printed on that same page (the row
lists the parts). Nothing here is calculated across pages. Where a company prints only a year-to-date total, that is
what the row says; the doubts list names the quarters that therefore could not be listed.

  on 29 Jul 2026 after a change to how some datacentre leases are classified, and about $170B on 28 Oct 2026. Each

  not because the build slowed.
