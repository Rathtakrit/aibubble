# Past bubbles: sourced numbers for comparison

Research file, 15 November 2026. Four earlier booms and busts, and two short ones, collected only from pages I
opened in this run. Every row has the date the source gives, the fact with its number, the URL I opened, the
publisher, and a Checked cell: `opened` means the page loaded in this run, `archive copy` means the live page
refused and I read the archived copy at web.archive.org.

Terms used below, explained once:

- **Price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio**: the share price divided by profit per share for a year. A high number means
  people are paying many years of current profit for a share.
- **CAPE**: a P/E that averages profit over ten years and adjusts for inflation, so one good year does not flatter it.
- **Dark fibre**: cable laid in the ground but never switched on, so it carries no traffic.
- **Vendor financing**: a supplier lending its customer the money to buy the supplier's own product.
- **Capacity swap**: two network owners each buy the same amount of capacity from the other. Each books a sale, and
  little or no money changes hands.
- **IRU (indefeasible right of use)**: a long lease on one path through a fibre network, sold as a one-off payment
  booked as revenue up front.
- **Chapter 11**: the United States bankruptcy process in which a company keeps running while it tries to pay
  creditors.

How many pages: 61 distinct pages opened in this run (36 Wikipedia articles read through the en.wikipedia.org
article endpoint, 23 other live pages, and 2 archived copies of sec.gov pages). Nine DuckDuckGo search pages were
opened only to find addresses; a search result on its own was never used as a source. Every page that refused a
fetch is listed at the end.

Units: money as the page prints it, in the currency it prints (US dollars unless marked £ or C$). Market values
dated 31 December come from a table headed "End of year Market Cap", so that is the date the page supports, not a
peak.
