By mid-2009 the bill is clear. Home prices fell about 30 percent on average from their mid-2006 peak to mid-2009, so the loans behind the bonds were worth less than the bonds and the insurance had to pay.

On 24 November 2008 a fund set up by the New York Fed and AIG paid Goldman Sachs $5.6 billion to end most of AIG's contracts: the cash AIG had promised, supplied by the state.

The $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program, passed on 3 October 2008, put public money behind the banks as well.
