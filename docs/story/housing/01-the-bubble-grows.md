Between 1998 and 2006 the mortgage debt on US homes rose from 61 percent of GDP to 97 percent. In 2006, 69 percent of households owned their home, and lenders made $2.8 trillion of new mortgages.

Countrywide alone made $468 billion of them that year.

House prices rose with the debt. The Case-Shiller 20-city index reached its peak of 206.52 in July 2006. While prices rose, a borrower who fell behind could refinance: a new, larger loan paid off the old one while the house was worth more, so the payments kept coming.
