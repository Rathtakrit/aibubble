Between 1998 and 2006 the mortgage debt on US homes rises from 61 percent of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year, to 97 percent. In 2006, 69 percent of households own their home, and lenders make $2.8 trillion of new mortgages.

Countrywide alone makes $468 billion of them that year.

House prices rise with the debt. Average US home prices more than double between 1998 and 2006, and the Case-Shiller 20-city index, a measure of house prices in 20 US cities, peaks in July 2006. While prices rise, a borrower who falls behind can refinance: a new, larger loan pays off the old one while the house is worth more, so the payments keep coming.
