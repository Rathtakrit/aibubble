Between 1998 and 2006, mortgage debt on US homes rises from 61 percent of GDP to 97 percent. GDP is everything the country produces in a year. In 2006, lenders make $2.8 trillion of mortgages, and Countrywide makes $468 billion. House prices more than double, and the Case-Shiller 20-city index peaks in July 2006. A borrower who falls behind refinances: a new, larger loan pays off the old one.
