A household pays interest and principal every month on its mortgage. The lender does not keep that loan. Countrywide, a big US mortgage lender, sells the loans on: $403 billion in 2006 alone, mostly in the form of securities.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are the biggest destination. Fannie's own book is $2.8 trillion, about 23 percent of US mortgage debt, at 30 September 2007.

A mortgage-backed security is a bond whose payments come from a pool of home loans. Investors hold the bond, the lender gets cash to lend again, and the household's monthly payment travels to a stranger. Banks hold these bonds too, and a bank that holds one can insure it.
