Countrywide does not keep its loans. It sells them, $403 billion in 2006, mostly as securities. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are the biggest destination. Fannie's book is $2.8 trillion, 23 percent of US mortgage debt. A bond is a loan split into pieces that pay investors interest. A mortgage-backed security is a bond paid by a pool of home loans. Investors pay cash for it, and the lender lends again. A bank that holds one can insure it.
