A household pays interest and principal, the amount borrowed, every month on its mortgage. The lender does not keep that loan. Countrywide, a big US mortgage lender, sells them on: $403 billion in 2006, mostly in the form of securities.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are the biggest destination. Fannie's book is $2.8 trillion, 23 percent of US mortgage debt.

A bond is a loan split into pieces that investors hold and that pays them interest. A mortgage-backed security is a bond whose payments come from a pool of home loans. Investors hold the bond, the lender gets cash to lend again, and the household's monthly payment travels to a stranger. A bank that holds one can insure it.
