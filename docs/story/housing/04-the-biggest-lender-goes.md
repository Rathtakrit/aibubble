Countrywide makes $40.6 billion of nonprime loans in 2006, and only $17 billion in 2007: mortgages to people with a poor credit record or no proof of income, charged a higher rate.

Those loans stay current only while prices rise. When prices stop, the refinancing route closes and the payments start to be missed.

On 11 January 2008 Bank of America agrees to take Countrywide for about $4 billion in stock. The lender that made the loans now sits inside a bank that has to live with them.
