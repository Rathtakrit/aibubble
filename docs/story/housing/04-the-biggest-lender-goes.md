Countrywide made $468 billion of mortgages in 2006, and $40.6 billion of them were nonprime: loans to people with a poor credit record or no proof of income, charged a higher rate.

Those loans only stay current while house prices rise, because a borrower in trouble can refinance into a new loan. When prices stop rising, the refinancing route closes and the payments start to be missed.

On 11 January 2008 Bank of America agrees to take Countrywide for about $4 billion in stock. The lender that made the loans now sits inside a bank that has to live with them.
