A credit default swap is insurance on a bond. Banks pay AIG a premium to insure bonds built from mortgages, and AIG promises to pay if the loans behind them default. At the end of 2007 AIG has insured $527 billion of them.

The contract has a second edge. If those bonds lose value, or AIG's own credit rating falls, the bank can demand collateral, cash held as security.

AIG treats the contracts as safe. On 26 July 2007 Goldman Sachs says a margin call, a demand for more cash, is on its way, and the next day sends an invoice for $1.8 billion. A promise to pay has become a demand for cash.
