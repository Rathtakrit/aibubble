Bear Stearns holds $102 billion of repo borrowing at the end of 2007. A repo is cash lent against bonds and repaid the next day. In June 2007 Merrill Lynch seizes $800 million of bonds that a Bear hedge fund had pledged against such loans.

On 13 March 2008, fearing losses on mortgage bonds, clients pull $10 billion of cash in a single day and trading partners call in their debts. Bear's reserves fall from $12.4 billion to $2 billion.

Three days later JPMorgan agrees to take the bank for $2 a share, $236 million, with the Federal Reserve funding up to $30 billion of the less liquid assets.
