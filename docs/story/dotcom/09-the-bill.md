Enron fails in December 2001 as its shares fall from about $95 to below $1. Global Crossing files in January 2002 with $12.4 billion of liabilities, the money it owes.

WorldCom admits on 25 June 2002 that it recorded about $3.8 billion of ordinary costs as investment rather than as expenses, which made its profit look bigger. It files for Chapter 11 on 21 July with $41 billion of debt.

The shares that paid for Time Warner and US West are worth less: the Nasdaq is 77% below its peak. The equipment makers' loans are losses. Shares used as money have lost their value, and loans made to win orders have gone bad.
