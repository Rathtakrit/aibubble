Carriers do not only trade capacity, the right to use a strand of fibre, with customers. They trade long leases on fibre with each other and count both sides as revenue. Qwest and Enron swap capacity: Qwest books $36.3 million from the Enron swaps in late 1999 and $85.5 million in the third quarter of 2001. Qwest also trades $105 million of capacity with Global Crossing in early 2001.

Global Crossing counts $720 million of "cash revenue" from its side of such swaps in the first half of 2001. No new customer appears. The same money moves around the industry and comes back as growth.
