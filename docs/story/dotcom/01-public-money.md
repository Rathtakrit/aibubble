Netscape makes the browser people use to reach the web. On 9 August 1995 it offers 5 million shares, parts of the company investors own, at $28 each. Demand lifts the price to $58.25 by the close, more than double, on a company with no profit.

Yahoo, a web directory, offers 2.6 million shares at $13 in April 1996 and closes its first day at $33. In December 1996 Alan Greenspan, the Federal Reserve chairman, asks if "irrational exuberance" has lifted asset values too far. On 14 May 1997 Amazon, an online bookshop, offers 3 million shares at $18 and raises about $54 million.

The public market pays for internet companies before they earn anything.
